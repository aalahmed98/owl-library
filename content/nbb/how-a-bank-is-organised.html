How a Bank Is Organised

Front, middle and back office — the standard structure of a mid-size Gulf commercial bank · Companion to the FTP and money markets papers · 2026

One caveat up front: banks rarely publish department-level structure — they publish segments (typically Retail, Corporate/Business Banking, and Investment & Treasury), which is a coarser cut. What follows is the standard structure for a bank of NBB's size and type: a mid-size Gulf commercial bank with retail, corporate, treasury and an Islamic arm. The names will differ slightly from any bank's internal labels, but the functions will all be there.

The grouping that actually explains how a bank works is front / middle / back office: who makes the money, who checks the risk, and who processes the result.

Front office — faces customers, generates revenue

Retail gathers the cheap stable deposits that quietly fund everything else; Corporate writes the big loans; Treasury & Investments — the unit the whole FTP series is about — manages the balance sheet between them and is often the second-largest profit source. Private banking, FI, trade finance, investment banking and the Islamic arm (at NBB, largely the majority-owned Bahrain Islamic Bank subsidiary) round out the revenue side.

Middle office — control and oversight, independent of the front

Risk (credit, market, liquidity/ALM, operational) challenges the deal-makers; Compliance keeps the bank inside CBB and international rules (AML/CFT is heavily resourced in Gulf banks because of correspondent-banking pressure); Credit Administration handles the mechanics after approval; Internal Audit deliberately reports to the Board, not the CEO, so it can criticise management; Finance owns the books — and the performance reports where FTP results actually land.

Back office — execution and processing

Operations settles payments and trades — rarely visible, catastrophic when it breaks. IT runs the core system and is increasingly the biggest capital spend; Information Security is kept deliberately separate from IT; Digital sits between IT and the business and gets reshuffled constantly.

Corporate functions

HR (which in Bahrain also owns regulatory Bahrainisation employment quotas), Strategy, Marketing, Investor Relations (NBB is listed on Bahrain Bourse), Procurement, and an increasingly standalone Sustainability/ESG function.
