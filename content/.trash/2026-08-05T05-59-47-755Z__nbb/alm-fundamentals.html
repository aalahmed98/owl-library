ALM Fundamentals

NBB research · Bank treasury series, part 4 of 4 · 2026

Asset–liability management is the discipline of keeping the structural mismatches of the balance sheet — in interest-rate terms and in cash terms — measured, limited, and deliberately chosen. Parts 1–3 built up to this: the bank earns a spread, the two sides of the balance sheet reprice differently, and FTP concentrates that mismatch into treasury's book. ALM is what treasury then does with it.

ALM manages two distinct risks that are easy to conflate:

  • Interest-rate risk in the banking book (IRRBB) — rates move and the bank's earnings or value change.
  • Liquidity risk — money is owed sooner than money comes in; the bank can be profitable and still die of a cash run.

1. The basic instrument: the repricing gap report

The workhorse of ALM is disarmingly simple: put every asset and liability into a time bucket by when its interest rate can next change (not when it matures — a floating-rate 20-year loan repricing every 3 months goes in the 0–3m bucket). Then compare the sides per bucket:

Gapbucket = rate-sensitive assets − rate-sensitive liabilities in that bucket.
Positive gap: more assets reprice → rising rates help. Negative gap: more liabilities reprice → rising rates hurt.

The toy bank below has 100m of assets, funded by 80m deposits + 10m wholesale + 10m equity. You control two behavioral dials — the same assumptions FTP wrestles with (part 3): how much of the loan book floats, and how much of the deposit base is "core" (sticky money that behaves long-term even though it could contractually leave tomorrow).

2. Two lenses on the same risk

Regulators (via the Basel IRRBB framework) require banks to look at rate risk through two complementary lenses, because each one misses what the other sees:

  • Earnings lens — ΔNII: if rates jump today, how much does net interest income change over the next 12 months? Driven by the short-bucket gaps. This is the CFO's view: next year's P&L.
  • Value lens — ΔEVE (economic value of equity): revalue every future cash flow at the new rates; how much does the present value of the whole balance sheet change? Long-dated fixed positions dominate, because their values swing hardest. This is the economist's view: the worth of the franchise.

The two can disagree violently. A bank loaded with long fixed bonds funded overnight might show rising NII in year one (assets yield more than funding costs move) while its economic value collapses — that combination is precisely what killed Silicon Valley Bank in 2023. How the toy model computes ΔNII and ΔEVE

ΔNII ≈ (gap0–3m × 1.0 + gap3–12m × 0.5) × Δr — each short-bucket gap earns/pays the new rate for roughly the fraction of the year it is repriced (full year for the 0–3m bucket, about half on average for 3–12m).

ΔEVE ≈ −Σ gapi × Di × Δr, with bucket durations D ≈ [0.125, 0.625, 3, 7] years. Duration measures how much a position's present value falls per 1% rate rise; long buckets dominate. Real ALM systems do full cash-flow revaluation with non-parallel scenarios — same idea, more machinery.

3. The liquidity side

Rate risk erodes; liquidity risk kills. The ALM function also owns the bank's cash survival horizon:

  • Liquidity buffer: a stock of high-quality liquid assets (HQLA — cash, government bonds) that can be sold or repo'd immediately in stress.
  • LCR (Liquidity Coverage Ratio): HQLA must cover ≥100% of modeled net cash outflows in a 30-day stress — the "survive one bad month" rule.
  • NSFR (Net Stable Funding Ratio): long-term assets must be backed by stable funding over a 1-year horizon — the "don't fund mortgages with hot money" rule.
  • Contingency funding plan: a rehearsed playbook of what gets sold, repo'd, or borrowed, in what order, when outflows exceed plan.

Note the deep link to FTP: the cost of holding these buffers is real money, and part 3's checklist is where it gets charged back to the products that cause the need for them.

4. The toolkit: what ALM actually does about mismatch

  • Natural hedging via steering: use FTP incentives and product design to attract the balance-sheet shape you want (e.g. promote floating-rate loans or longer term deposits).
  • Interest-rate swaps: the workhorse hedge. Pay-fixed/receive-floating swaps shorten the asset side synthetically; receive-fixed swaps do the opposite. Most IRRBB is managed with swap overlays rather than by changing the customer book.
  • The investment portfolio: the securities book doubles as a duration-adjustment tool and the liquidity buffer.
  • Term funding: issuing bonds/term deposits to fix funding costs and satisfy NSFR.
  • Limits: hard caps on gap sizes, ΔNII and ΔEVE sensitivities, and liquidity ratios — the guardrails inside which the businesses may run.

5. The process: how ALM runs month to month

ALM is a governance cycle, not a spreadsheet. The recurring loop at nearly every bank:

  1. Measure. The ALM/treasury middle office produces the monthly pack: repricing gaps, ΔNII and ΔEVE under standard shocks (±2% parallel, steepener, flattener…), LCR/NSFR, funding concentration, behavioral model updates.
  2. Review at ALCO. The Asset–Liability Committee — typically CFO (chair), treasurer, chief risk officer, heads of business lines — meets monthly. It owns the balance-sheet risk appetite on the board's behalf.
  3. Decide. ALCO sets or amends: hedge instructions to treasury, the FTP curve and any strategic overlays (part 3's checklist is effectively ALCO's agenda item), deposit pricing guidance, funding plan, limit changes.
  4. Execute. Treasury's front office transacts — swaps, bond issuance, securities purchases — within the delegated limits.
  5. Monitor and escalate. Risk (a separate, independent function) checks positions against limits daily; breaches escalate to ALCO or the board. Stress tests and behavioral-model backtests feed the next cycle.

That closes the loop of this series: the spread (part 1) is earned by pricing two very different rates (part 2), split fairly and de-risked at the business level by FTP (part 3), leaving one central mismatch that ALM measures, limits, and hedges through the ALCO cycle (this part).
