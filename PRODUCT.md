# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

One owner (the admin), who adds and maintains everything, and guests the owner shares a read-only password with, mainly students in a bank treasury department. Desktop-first, long sessions reading study documents (interactive HTML lessons, markdown notes, PDFs). The owner may also connect Claude via MCP. Occasional viewing on the go.

## Product Purpose

owl-library is a private research library: plain files under `content/`, a live reader/editor website, full-text search, and two roles: an admin who can change anything and guests who can only read. Success looks like: finding any doc in seconds, reading comfortably for long stretches, and the site feeling like a considered, personal instrument rather than a generic file browser.

## Brand Personality

Precise, quiet, studious. A well-kept study desk: warm paper, a clear index, and lessons that look like they belong to the place they are kept. Not the gloss of a SaaS product. The owl is the mark: watchful and exact, never cute.

## Anti-references

- Playful consumer apps: no bubbly rounded UI, no emoji-driven chrome, no gamification.
- Generic SaaS dashboards: no KPI tiles, no gradient CTAs, no marketing hero patterns.
- Default-blue Bootstrap-flavored tool UI (what the first version looked like).

## Design Principles

1. **The catalog is the aesthetic.** Lists, indexes, and tabular metadata are the native visual language of a library; lean into them instead of card grids.
2. **Documents are the star.** Chrome stays quiet on reading surfaces; identity moments (sign-in, home) carry the brand.
3. **One accent, used with intent.** A single distinctive accent color carries wayfinding and identity; everything else is tinted ink and paper.
4. **Same bones on every page.** One header pattern, one list pattern, one form pattern, one badge system, reused everywhere.
5. **Fast and self-contained.** No external requests; vendored fonts and assets; instant loads on localhost and over the tunnel.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA contrast in both themes, full keyboard navigability with visible focus states, `prefers-reduced-motion` respected. No known specific user needs beyond that.
